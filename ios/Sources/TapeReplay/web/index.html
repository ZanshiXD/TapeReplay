How orders fill

Prices in the file are treated as bid. Each base bar is replayed as a price path, open → low → high → close on up bars and open → high → low → close on down bars, and every order, stop and target fills where that path crosses it. Gaps between bars fill at the next open. Times are shown in UTC.

How the simulation works

Runs one account through its whole course in this session. Pass the evaluation and a fresh funded account opens at the same size. Each funded payout comes out of the balance and locks the max loss; after the last one the account moves to live. Live opens with no profit and the live max loss; live payouts can be taken any day. Breaking a max loss ends that account; start a new evaluation from the card, and its fee is counted. The max loss trails the end-of-day high and locks at start + the lock amount once the end-of-day balance is that far above the max loss (live: once profit reaches the max loss).

Functions you write

on_bar(ctx, bar) runs when each candle of the strategy timeframe closes. bar has time, open, high, low, close, volume and index; time is the candle's open in the chart's time zone.

on_tick(ctx, tick) runs on every simulated tick when you choose Every tick: tick.time, tick.price.

Optional: init(ctx) before the first bar, on_fill(ctx, fill) after each fill (side, qty, price, kind entry or exit, reason, pnl), and on_end(ctx) at the end.

Orders

ctx.buy(qty, sl=None, tp=None) and ctx.sell(…) trade at market; add limit=price or stop=price for a working order. Also buy_limit, sell_limit, buy_stop, sell_stop(price, qty, sl, tp). Leave out qty to use the size in Session settings. Stop loss and take profit are prices. Each returns the order's id, or None if it was rejected (the console says why).

ctx.close() closes the position at market, ctx.cancel(id) and ctx.cancel_all() cancel working orders, ctx.set_bracket(sl=…, tp=…) moves the open position's stop and target.

How fills work

Market orders and close() fill on the next tick: from on_bar that is the next bar's open, so a strategy cannot trade at a price it has already seen. Limit and stop orders, stop losses and take profits fill inside bars, exactly as in manual replay. Size, costs, spread, slippage, point value and the prop simulation come from Session settings.

What the strategy can read

ctx.closes(n), opens, highs, lows, volumes, times: the last n closed candles (all of them without n) as numpy arrays, oldest first. times are UTC epoch seconds.

ctx.df(n): the same candles as a pandas DataFrame (choose numpy + pandas). Building one on every bar is slow over long runs; numpy arrays are much faster.

ctx.position (+ long, − short, 0 flat), ctx.position_price, ctx.equity, ctx.balance, ctx.time, ctx.price, ctx.orders, ctx.trades, ctx.history.

ctx.tick_size, ctx.ticks(n) (n ticks as a price distance), ctx.bid and ctx.ask (sells fill at the bid, buys at the ask), ctx.spread, ctx.round(price) (to the data's precision), ctx.point_value, ctx.at("09:30") (a time to compare with bar.time.time()). Keep your own values on ctx, like ctx.count = 0.

Optimize

The Optimize tab lists the NAME = value settings at the top of your code (numbers, True/False and text choices; times of day are left out). Tick the ones to try, adjust the values, and it runs every combination, several at a time, ranked by your goal. A setting can also carry its own range: FAST = param(9, 5, 30, step=5). Check on the last leaves the end of the dates out of the ranking and shows how each combination did there: a result that only works on the dates it was tuned on is luck. On iPhone and iPad it runs one or two at a time, up to 300 combinations, and pauses if you leave the app (tap Resume when you are back).

Good to know

print() writes to the console. Errors stop the run and show the line. A run is a new session: it is saved to the Library and opens on the chart, and the session you had stays in the Library.

At bar close is fast. Every tick calls Python many times per bar and is slower; the ticks are simulated from each bar's open, high, low and close. A strategy that never returns stops the app responding.

Z shows or hides them

Zones come only from finished candles of the timeframe chosen here. FVG: the gap between candles one and three. iFVG: an FVG a candle closed through, now facing the other way. BPR: a new FVG overlapping an opposite one still open. OB: after a close beyond the last swing, the candle with the extreme low (or high) since that swing. BB: an order block a candle closed through. iFVGs and breakers keep drawing from where they formed, with a dashed mark where they flipped.
